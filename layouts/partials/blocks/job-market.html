Environmental Regulation, Firms, and Trade:
Evidence from Mexico’s Air-Quality Programs

How does local environmental regulation affect firm dynamics and spatial welfare? I study Mexico’s ProAire programs using comprehensive linked firm-level registry data and a quantitative spatial general equilibrium model that jointly accounts for international trade, atmospheric pollution transport, and worker migration sorting.

Teaching

Indiana University Bloomington · Boise State University

I have served as the primary instructor for international economics, alongside leading discussion sections for microeconomics, advanced microeconomic theory, and behavioral economics. My pedagogical objective is to cultivate economic intuition through a structured four-part framework:

  1. Articulate the core economic question precisely.
  2. Understand the underlying structural mechanisms.
  3. Examine the empirical and causal evidence.
  4. Discuss actionable policy implications.

INEGI Data Access

Institutional research resource for IU scholars

Graduate students and faculty researchers with Indiana University affiliation conducting empirical research on Mexico can apply for secure remote access to restricted administrative microdata provided by Mexico's national statistical agency, the Instituto Nacional de Estadística y Geografía (INEGI), through the established IU–INEGI collaborative agreement.
