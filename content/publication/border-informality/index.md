title: "Insourcing the Workforce: Labor-Market Adjustment to Mexico's Subcontracting Reform"
  bstract: "Mexico’s April 2021 reform prohibited the supply of workers by one company to another
and forced firms to move agency workers onto their own payrolls. I study how employ
ment, hours, and pay adjusted using monthly industry panels covering manufacturing as
a whole (EMIM, 2017–2023) and the export-oriented manufacturing program (IMMEX,
2017–2026), and I corroborate the results with a panel of about 134,000 establishments from
the Economic Census. The research design compares industries that entered the reform
with different pre-determined shares of supplied workers. Exposed industries eliminated
roughly four-fifths or more of their supplied labor and recorded payroll employment
gains of 12–13 log points per 10 percentage points of exposure, yet total employment and
total hours did not change detectably. An accounting benchmark shows that one-for-one
conversion of supplied workers into payroll workers explains about 90 percent of the
payroll gain and reproduces the absence of a total-employment response. Average pay
per worker did not rise in industries more exposed to the reform, and the longer IMMEX
series suggests a modest decline that is sensitive to industry-specific trends. Evidence
on production and exports is imprecise. The reform therefore moved workers across
employment statuses on a large scale without creating or destroying jobs in net terms, a
pattern that employment counts alone would misread as job creation.'
