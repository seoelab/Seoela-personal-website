abstract: "How does the choice of which fuels to tax affect the cost of reducing emissions? I
study Mexico’s transition from fuel subsidies to excise and carbon taxation. The carbon
levy exempted natural gas as access to imported gas expanded in the power sector. Us-
ing Mexico’s Economic Census, I compare manufacturers with similar initial energy cost
shares but different exposure to the legislated tax schedule because of their reliance on
fuel. A one-standard-deviation increase in exposure corresponds to 4.8 percent lower
fuel spending among plants reporting fuel purchases and a 0.28-percentage-point in-
crease in electricity’s share of operating costs. Output falls by 4.6 percent in the un-
weighted comparison, while employment changes little. In a quantitative trade model,
the enacted schedule reduces domestic emissions by 5.6 percent at a real consumption
cost of $271 per tonne of CO2 avoided. At the same net revenue, a levy proportional to
carbon content across all fuels more than doubles abatement and lowers the cost to $159
per tonne. Extending carbon-based rates to natural gas accounts for $69 of the $112 sav-
ing per tonne."
