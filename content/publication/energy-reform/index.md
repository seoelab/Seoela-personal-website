abstract: "Mexico is one of a few middle-income countries to reform its fuel subsidies, moving from a net subsidy burden of 1.8 percent of GDP in 2008 to fuel-tax revenue of 1.6 percent of GDP in 2018. Using Economic Census establishments from 2008 to 2023, this paper studies the effect of the reforms by comparing plants by their pre-reform fossil fuel cost shares. The results show that a one standard deviation increase in exposure to the reform  reduces a plant's fuel cost share by 2.0 percentage points, and it causes a 4.6 percent reduction in average plant output, with no effect on manufacturing CO2emissions. In a calibrated small open-economy model, the actual reform package abates 19.8 million tonnes of CO2 at a cost of $129 per tonne, mostly in transport, households, refining, and electricity. A uniform carbon-carbon intensity based tax on all fuels that raises the same amount of revenue would reduce this to $69 per tonne and abate 42.4 million tonnes."
