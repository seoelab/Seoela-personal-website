abstract:  "Air-quality policy often targets the areas of the highest pollution exposure, inducing spatial heterogeneity in abatement costs and environmental benefits. This paper studies the effects of one such policy, Mexico's ProAire air-quality programs, on manufacturing emissions, trade, and consumer welfare. I utilize the staggered rollout of ProAire across municipalities from 2011 to 2020 combined with matched firm-level data. I find that the decrease in the emissions intensity of Mexican manufacturing between 2005 and 2018 was mostly due to within-industry technique changes (88 percent). Following ProAire adoption, emissions from exposed firms decrease by 11 percent. The trade response is mainly along the extensive and intensive export margins: with no change in export entries, a 2.8 percentage points increase in export exits, and a 9.5 percent decrease in export sales for the surviving exporters. Among the surviving importers, import volume increased by 18 percent. Using a quantitative spatial model of heterogeneous firms, pollution-intensive production, trade, atmospheric transport, and worker sorting, I find that ProAire has a small negative effect on output of 0.07 percent but improves welfare by 0.099 percent."
