I have served as the primary instructor for an international economics course as well as a teaching assistant in microeconomics, advanced microeconomic theory, and behavioral economics. I strive to teach students how to think like economists by introducing a basic process to analyze public policy questions. In particular, I guide students to a) articulate the economic question, b) understand the mechanisms, c) examine the evidence, and d) discuss policy implications.
